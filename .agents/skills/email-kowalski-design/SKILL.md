# Email Kowalski Design

## Overview

Use this skill for email/report surfaces that need to feel crisp, serious, and immediately useful. "Kowalski" means high-signal, report-first design: compact, structured, restrained, and easy to scan under time pressure.

## Workflow

1. Inspect existing report/email generation files before editing.
2. Decide what the reader needs in the first 10 seconds: portfolio health, biggest movers, risk flags, actions, and data freshness.
3. Build the email around a strong information hierarchy: headline summary, key metrics, holdings/movers, risk notes, then details.
4. Prefer simple, email-safe structure. Avoid JavaScript, fragile layout tricks, external fonts, and complex responsive behavior that will fail in email clients.
5. Make numbers easy to compare with aligned columns, restrained color coding, and explicit currency labels.
6. Clearly label stale, partial, failed, or unavailable data.
7. Preserve educational-only language. Never make the email sound like trading advice or guaranteed recommendations.

## Visual Language

- Use a quiet, executive-report feel rather than a promotional newsletter.
- Keep the palette restrained but not lifeless: neutral base, one accent, clear positive/negative colors.
- Use compact sections with strong headings and enough whitespace to breathe.
- Make tables readable on narrow screens by prioritizing the most important columns.
- Use plain-language labels over market jargon when possible.
- Keep alerts short, direct, and visually distinct without shouting.

## HTML Email Checks

Before handoff, check:

- key content appears without relying on images
- links and buttons have clear text
- colors have readable contrast
- financial values include currency labels
- fallback text is honest when data is missing
- no unsupported CSS or interactive behavior is required for comprehension
